About

Thihan Chandramohan

Physiotherapist. More than twenty years in practice, more than fifteen in elite sport, most of it in cricket.

What I do

I assess and rehabilitate cricket injuries, and I help teams and organisations build the systems that decide whether players stay available. Those two jobs sound separate. They are the same job at different scales.

Most of my week is spent one to one with cricketers at Bridge Road Physiotherapy in Richmond, or on video calls with players elsewhere. The rest is consulting work: injury surveillance, workload systems, rehabilitation pathways, and reviews of how a sports science and sports medicine department operates.

Where I have worked

How I work

Three things shape how I approach an injury.

Availability is the outcome that matters

A player who is fit in the gym and unavailable on Saturday has not been rehabilitated. So the plan works backwards from the match: what the role demands, what the schedule looks like, and what has to be true before you take the field. Everything else is intermediate.

Simple systems survive contact with a season

Elaborate programmes look thorough and fail quietly. Players stop doing them in week three, usually without telling anyone. I would rather give you four things you will do than fourteen you will not. The same applies to team systems: the monitoring nobody fills in is worse than none, because it produces confident decisions built on gaps.

Evidence matters, context decides

The research tells you what tends to happen across groups. It does not tell you what to do with a bowler who has one match left in a season, a contract in question, and a coach who wants him playing. Those constraints are real, and pretending otherwise produces plans nobody follows. I will tell you what the evidence supports, where it runs out, and what I would do given your situation.

What I will not do

I will not promise you a timeline I cannot stand behind. Tissue heals at its own rate and the honest answer to "how long" is often a range with conditions attached.

I will not tell you an injury is caused by one thing. Cricket injuries almost never are. Bowling load matters, and so do sleep, travel, schedule density, technique, previous injury and what happened in the gym.

I will not sell you a screening product that claims to predict injury. No screening test does that reliably. Testing is useful for setting a baseline and tracking change, which is a smaller and more honest claim.

Cricket, from the other side

I have had my own surgery and rehabilitation, which is a useful thing for a physiotherapist to have been through. It shortens the distance between what a plan looks like on paper and what it feels like at week six when progress has stopped.

The two practices

The Cricket Physio is the cricket side of my work. Bridge Road Physiotherapy is the Richmond clinic, and it treats everything: backs, shoulders, running injuries, gym injuries and the general run of things people arrive with. Same physiotherapist, same room. If your injury has nothing to do with cricket, Bridge Road is the front door.
