About

Thihan Chandramohan

Physiotherapist. I work with cricketers one to one, and with the programs that manage them.

More than twenty years in physiotherapy

Fifteen of those years have been in high-performance sport, most of it cricket. State, franchise, county and international sides, and the pathway programs underneath them.

I assess and rehabilitate cricket injuries, and I help teams and organisations build the systems that decide whether players stay available. Those two jobs sound separate. They are the same job at different scales.

Most of my week is spent one to one with cricketers, in person in Melbourne or on video with players elsewhere. The rest is consulting: injury surveillance, workload systems, rehabilitation pathways, and reviews of how a sports science and sports medicine department runs.

I have had my own surgery and rehabilitation, which is a useful thing for a physiotherapist to have been through. It shortens the distance between what a plan looks like on paper and what it feels like at week six, when progress has stopped.

How I work

Availability is the outcome that matters

A player who is fit in the gym and unavailable on Saturday has not been rehabilitated. So the plan works backwards from the match: what the role demands, what the schedule looks like, and what has to be true before you take the field.

Simple systems survive contact with a season

Elaborate programs look thorough and fail quietly. Players stop doing them in week three, usually without telling anyone. I would rather give you four things you will do than fourteen you will not. The same goes for team systems: monitoring nobody fills in is worse than none.

Evidence matters, context decides

Research tells you what tends to happen across groups. It does not tell you what to do with a bowler who has one match left, a contract in question and a coach who wants them playing. I tell you what the evidence supports, where it runs out, and what I would do in your situation.

What I will not do. Promise a timeline I cannot stand behind. Sell a screening test that claims to predict injury, because no screening test does that reliably.
