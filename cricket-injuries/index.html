The pattern behind most cricket injuries

Cricket does not produce a random spread of injuries. It produces a fairly predictable set, because the game loads bodies in a fairly predictable way: repeated high-speed bowling, hard throwing from the outfield, repeated sprints between wickets, and long days that put all of it under fatigue.

Two things follow from that.

The first is that most cricket injuries are load problems rather than accidents. A hamstring that tears sprinting a third run rarely tore because of that run. It tore because of what the previous six weeks did to its capacity.

The second is that the schedule is part of the diagnosis. A bowler with back pain and eleven days before the next match is a different clinical problem to the same bowler with a match on Saturday, even though the back is identical. Any plan that ignores the fixture list is a plan for somebody else.

That is why every guide here spends as much time on what has been happening around the injury as on the injury itself.
