About appointments and how this works

Do I have to play at a high level to be seen?

No. Most of the people I see play club, school or pathway cricket. The reasoning is the same whatever level you play: work out what the tissue tolerates, work out what your role demands, and close the gap.

Do you see junior cricketers?

Yes, and a parent or guardian is welcome in the room. Young fast bowlers are the group where back pain matters most, because growing spines under bowling load behave differently to adult spines.

Do you see recreational cricketers?

Yes. A Saturday afternoon bowler with a sore back has the same injury as a professional with a sore back. The difference is the schedule around it, not the tissue.

Where are Melbourne appointments?

Bridge Road Physiotherapy, 507 Bridge Road, Richmond VIC 3121, inside Uplift Gym. No gym membership is needed. Enter through the main gym entrance.

How does Bridge Road Physiotherapy relate to The Cricket Physio?

Same physiotherapist, same room, two front doors. Bridge Road is the Richmond clinic and treats everything, sport and everyday. The Cricket Physio is the cricket side of that work, plus telehealth, consulting and education. In-person appointments for both are booked through the Bridge Road calendar.

How does telehealth work?

A video call, 45 minutes for a first appointment. I take a history, watch you move through tests I set up on camera, and send a written plan afterwards. It suits workload problems, stalled rehabilitation, return to bowling and second opinions.

What should I bring to an appointment?

Shorts or clothing you are able to move in. Your cricket shoes if the problem involves bowling or running. Any scans or reports you have. Rough figures for what you have been bowling over the past six weeks, which is more useful than most people expect.

Do I need a referral?

No. You are able to book directly. A referral is only needed if you are claiming under a Medicare care plan or a compensable scheme, in which case your doctor arranges it.

Do you treat injuries that have nothing to do with cricket?

Yes, through Bridge Road Physiotherapy: backs, necks, shoulders, knees, running and gym injuries, and the general run of things.

Do you consult to teams and organisations?

Yes. Injury surveillance, bowling workload systems, rehabilitation and return-to-play pathways, and reviews of sports science and sports medicine departments. Use the team enquiry form rather than the booking calendar.

Do you work with interstate or overseas players?

Regularly, by telehealth. Time zones are workable, and a good share of that work is with players on tour or in programmes where local support is thin.

Are you able to tell me what is wrong by email?

No. Diagnosing an injury without a history and an examination would be guessing, and guessing about an injury is how people end up bowling through something that needed stopping. Book a short appointment instead.

Do you do injury screening that predicts who will get hurt?

No, because no screening test does that reliably. Testing is useful for setting a baseline and measuring change against the demands of your role, which is a smaller and more honest claim.

What are your fees?

Telehealth is $150 for an initial consultation and $120 for a review. In-person and Return to Performance fees, private health claiming and Medicare care plans are published on the Bridge Road Physiotherapy fees page, which is the single source of truth for pricing.
