Common problems

What cricketers usually arrive with

Cricket does not produce mysterious injuries. It produces a fairly predictable set, driven by how the game loads a body: repeated bowling, hard throwing, sprinting between wickets, and long days.

Fast bowling back pain

Lower back pain in bowlers, including bone stress injury of the lumbar spine. The injury that costs young quicks the most cricket.

Background

Why cricket specifically

I have been a physiotherapist for more than twenty years, and worked in elite sport for more than fifteen of them. Most of that has been cricket.

Roles have included work with Cricket Victoria, Cricket Australia, the Melbourne Stars and Melbourne Renegades, Hampshire Cricket, Sri Lanka Cricket and the Bangladesh Cricket Board. I am Head of Sports Science and Sports Medicine with the Federazione Cricket Italiana, and I sit on the Victorian State Council of Sports Medicine Australia.

None of that makes a hamstring heal faster. What it does buy is pattern recognition. I have watched a lot of bowlers through a lot of seasons, which means I have a reasonable idea of which problems settle with time, which ones are being made worse by the schedule, and what a coach will realistically agree to.

Tool

The Bowling Workload Tool

A mobile-first tool for logging bowling exposure, planning sessions and noticing patterns in workload and soreness over a season.

It does not predict injuries, and it does not pretend to. It shows you what you have bowled, which is the part most bowlers and most coaches are guessing at.

Open the workload tool

Access is by request rather than open signup, so the tool stays useful and supported.

Who it is for

Bowlers who want a record instead of a memory. Coaches who want one shared number for the squad. Physiotherapists who want to see what happened in the four weeks before the pain started.

Common questions

Do I have to play at a high level to be seen?

No. Most people I see play club, school or pathway cricket. The thinking is the same whatever level you play: work out what the tissue will tolerate, work out what your role demands, and close the gap between the two.

Do you see junior cricketers?

Yes, and a parent or guardian is welcome in the room. Young fast bowlers are the group where back pain matters most, because growing spines under bowling load behave differently to adult spines.

Where are in-person appointments?

At Bridge Road Physiotherapy, 507 Bridge Road, Richmond, inside Uplift Gym. That is my clinic. You book through the Bridge Road site and you see me there.

How does telehealth work?

A video call, usually 45 minutes for a first appointment. I take a history, watch you move through tests I set up on camera, and we build a plan you leave with in writing. It suits workload problems, rehab that has stalled and second opinions.

Do you treat injuries that have nothing to do with cricket?

Yes, through Bridge Road Physiotherapy. The Cricket Physio is the cricket side of the same practice, not a separate clinic.
