Return to performance

Pain-free is a checkpoint, not the finish line.

Most reinjuries happen to players who felt ready. Pain settles well before capacity returns, so rehab here runs until you are prepared for the demands of cricket again.

  1. Settle and rebuild

    Symptoms under control, capacity coming back.

    • Symptoms
    • Physical capacity
    • Running

  2. Return to participation

    Back in cricket training, modified, not yet ready to play.

    • Cricket movement
    • Skills
    • Training

  3. Return to sport

    Playing matches, not yet at your previous level.

    • Competition

  4. Return to performance

    Playing at or above your level before the injury.

    • Performance

The three return phases follow the 2016 consensus statement on return to sport (Ardern and colleagues, British Journal of Sports Medicine).

Fast bowlers

Fast bowlers get hurt differently. They need managing differently.

Lumbar bone stress, side strains, hamstrings, and the shoulders and ankles that take the landing. Most of it is decided by how bowling load changes, not by any single spell.

Ball counts matter, and they are not the whole story. Intensity, long gaps, growth, sleep, travel and previous injury all change what the same overs do to a body. The work is reading those together.

Consultations

What happens when you book

  1. Before

    A short questionnaire on your cricket, the injury and your schedule, so the appointment starts further along.

  2. Assessment

    History in cricket terms: what you bowled, when the pain arrives in a spell, what the next six weeks hold. Then testing against what your role demands.

  3. A written plan

    What to do, what to stop, the milestones, and what has to be true before each step.

  4. Your team

    With your consent, I talk to your coach, S&C or treating physio. The plan works alongside your support team, not instead of it.
