The problems I get called in for

When a squad carries more injuries than it should, the cause is rarely missing equipment or an untried treatment. It is usually one of these.

  • Load nobody records

    Bowling load is estimated from memory, which flatters everyone.

  • Return-to-play decisions nobody owns

    Coach, physio and player each assume someone else has it. It defaults to whoever is most confident in the room.

  • Rehab that stops at pain-free

    Players return without testing against the demands of their role, and the reinjury gets called bad luck.

  • Information lost at handovers

    Between seasons, between staff, between club and pathway. Every new person starts from zero.

  • Monitoring nobody fills in

    Worse than none, because the gaps look like zeroes.

None of these needs new software. The work is deciding who decides what, writing it down, and keeping the system small enough that busy people use it.

Three types of engagement

Audit

A department review

A structured look at how your sports science and sports medicine department runs: structure, communication, injury trends, reporting, monitoring, staffing, scheduling and rehab pathways.

You get a written report with prioritised recommendations, not a list of everything that is imperfect.

Program build

Surveillance, workload, rehab pathways, return-to-play criteria

Injury surveillance and workload recording that people keep up with, answering the questions you keep asking.

Written return-to-play criteria for the injuries that cost your squad the most cricket, with who owns each decision. Built for the staff you have.

Tournament support

Planning, medical logistics, on-tour physio

Medical planning and logistics before you travel, and physiotherapy on tour.

Built for places where local support is thin and the documentation matters more than the equipment.

How it runs

  1. Scoping call

    What you have tried, why it stopped, and whether I am the right person. Sometimes the honest answer is that you do not need me.

  2. Written proposal

    Scope, deliverables, timeline and fee in writing, so everyone agrees to the same thing before work starts.

  3. Delivery

    The smallest thing that would help, first. A squad with no load monitoring needs one shared sheet running for a season before anyone discusses software.

  4. Handover

    Written down and owned by your staff, so it survives when people change. Reviews name structures, not individuals.
