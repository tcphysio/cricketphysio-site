What telehealth suits, and where it stops

Works well on video

  • Backs and shoulders that flare with bowling volume
  • Rehab that has stopped moving forward
  • Building and adjusting a return-to-bowling plan
  • A second opinion on a diagnosis or plan you already have
  • Players on tour with thin local support

Needs someone in the room

  • A new injury that needs examining
  • Severe pain with no clear cause
  • Anything that needs imaging or a doctor

If your problem needs hands-on assessment, I tell you in the consult and point you to an in-person physio or a doctor near you.

What happens in session one

  1. History, in cricket terms

    What you have bowled, thrown and batted. Your role in the side. The next six weeks of fixtures.

  2. Testing against your role

    Movement, strength and tolerance, checked against what a bowler, keeper or batter needs.

  3. A written plan

    What to do, what to stop, the milestones, and what has to be true before the next step.

Juniors and parents

A parent or guardian is welcome in the room or on the call. Back pain in a young fast bowler needs early assessment, because a growing spine responds to bowling load differently to an adult spine.
