Telehealth

Cricket physiotherapy by video

For cricketers who are not in Melbourne. A proper appointment, not a chat: history, tested movement, and a written plan you leave with.

Book a telehealth appointment Is it right for my problem

What telehealth suits

In cricket, a surprising amount of the useful work needs no hands. The diagnosis usually comes out of the history: what you bowled, when the pain arrives in a spell, what the schedule has done recently. That conversation works as well on video as in a room.

Telehealth works well for:

  • Workload problems. Backs and shoulders that flare with volume rather than with one event.
  • Rehabilitation that has stalled. You are six weeks in, progress stopped, and nobody has changed the plan.
  • Return to bowling. Building and adjusting a progression week by week, which is monitoring work rather than hands-on work.
  • Second opinions. You have a diagnosis and a plan, and you want someone who works in cricket to look at it.
  • Players on tour or overseas with thin local support.

What it does not suit

An acute injury that needs examining. A first episode of severe pain with no clear cause. Anything where the honest answer is that I need to put hands on it or you need imaging. If you book and it turns out to be one of these, I will tell you in the appointment and sort out the fee rather than carry on regardless.

How the appointment runs

  1. Before. You get a short questionnaire about your cricket, the injury and your schedule. Fill it in and the appointment starts further along.
  2. History. Roughly the first half. Cricket-specific, detailed, and the part that does most of the work.
  3. Testing on camera. I set up movement and strength tests you perform while I watch. Less than a physical examination, more than nothing, and enough for most of what comes up.
  4. The plan. Built with you, so it fits your week rather than an ideal one. Sent afterwards in writing.
  5. Review. Most people need one or two. Some need none.

Common questions

What do I need for the call?

A phone or laptop, a few metres of space, and somewhere to prop the camera. If you have a resistance band, a step and a ball, have them nearby. I will tell you beforehand if anything else helps.

Is telehealth worth it for an injury you cannot touch?

For some problems, no, and I will say so and refund rather than waste your time. For workload questions, stalled rehabilitation, technique-adjacent pain and second opinions, the examination adds less than the history and the plan do.

Do I get anything in writing?

Yes. A written plan after every appointment: what to do, what to stop, the milestones, and what has to be true before you progress.

Are you able to claim on private health?

Telehealth physiotherapy rebates depend on your fund and your policy. Check with your fund before booking. Bridge Road issues the receipt you need either way.

What if I am overseas or on tour?

That is a large share of the telehealth work. Time zones are workable. Tell me the schedule when you book and I will find a time that is not the middle of your night.
